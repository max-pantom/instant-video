<instant-video> · MIT · zero dependencies in the browser

Video that starts in ~20 ms.

On a normal <video> a hover pays for connecting, fetching, parsing, creating a decoder and decoding the first frame. instant-video does all of that before the pointer arrives, so the hover only has to press play.

Usage

Hover a card to preview; click to expand the same element into the full player. The number is pointer-enter → playing.Tap a card to expand its preview into the full player.

Measuring starts on your first hover. Twelve cards reuse four synthetic test-pattern clips, tinted.

02 · the race

Same clip, two engines.

Both players start on the same gesture. The plain <video> sets its src at that moment (cache-busted, so every run is a cold fetch of the 515 KB progressive file). The instant-video side already holds a decoded frame 0.

Cursor prediction

The engine projects the pointer ~180 ms ahead (velocity over the last 24–140 ms) and warms the card it is heading to. Turn the overlay on and sweep across the cards.

Pointer only. On touch there is no path to project; a touch warms the card on pointerdown.

04 · bench

Measured, not promised.

Chromium, desktop, fast link. 1440×900, no throttling, 24 hovers per scenario on distinct cards, median ms from pointer-enter, 2026-11-02 against museum-of-ads.vercel.app. Old engine is the plain <video> preview it replaced.

old new

Cells are median / p95 ms. cold = fresh browser context · warm = HTTP cache primed · predicted = curved decelerating mouse path with prediction on · unpredicted = teleport onto the card with prediction off · noprefetch = idle head prefetch off. Source: museum-of-ads docs/bench-results.md. A 25 fps clip cannot show a new frame sooner than 40 ms after start. Without prefetch and prediction, a cold unpredicted hover is no faster than the old engine (144 vs 146 ms to playing).
